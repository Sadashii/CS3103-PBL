    How to read this indicator

    What it is. A model trained on 10 years of JPM 1-minute data looks at each 5-minute candle as it closes and says what is likely in the next 10 minutes and for the rest of the day. A past day is played back minute by minute (pick it under Day, press ▶ Play). The model only sees candles that have already closed, and nothing it said is ever changed afterwards.

    The chart shows the whole day, 9:30 to 16:00

    • Left of the last candle is what happened. Right of it is the prediction up to the close.
    • Rest of day: the thick blue line is the expected price; the dashed lines are a likely high and low (the close lands between them about 9 days in 10).
    • Next 10 min: a short yellow band after the last candle. If the call is Flat, the price should stay inside it.
    • Outlooks: closing ranges fixed at 10:00 (blue) and 12:30 (purple), checked at the close.
    • Past calls: ▲ Up / ▼ Down calls made earlier today, with ✓ or ✗ once their 10 minutes are over.
    • Levels: green lines are support below the price, red lines resistance above (yesterday's high/low/close, the 9:30-10:00 opening range, today's high/low, pivots, swing points).
    • A dimmed candle is still forming. Calls are only made on finished candles.

    The calls

    • Up / Flat / Down: will the price move more than about 0.06% in the next 10 minutes, and which way. Guessing gets 33%; the model gets about 41% on two years of data it never saw.
    • Activity (Quiet / Normal / Active) and Big move risk say how much the price is likely to move. This is the model's strongest skill.
    • Which direction a move goes is close to a coin flip. "Move expected, slight Up lean" means: expect movement, don't bet on the direction.

    When not to trust it

    • On a single day the model can lose to "always flat" (about 1 day in 5). The Signal log scorecard shows how it is doing today against simple rules.
    • Around news, earnings or Fed announcements, moves can be bigger than any range shown.
    • It is not a trading system: trading every Up/Down call loses money after costs.
